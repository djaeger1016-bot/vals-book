# Teacher's Guide

**Doodles and the Backyard Birds** is an excellent resource for classroom learning. This guide provides discussion prompts, activities, and learning objectives to enhance the reading experience for your students.

---

## Learning Objectives

After reading **Doodles and the Backyard Birds**, students will be able to:

✓ Understand the importance of friendship and loyalty  
✓ Recognize acts of bravery, both big and small  
✓ Identify how teamwork helps overcome challenges  
✓ Appreciate differences and unique qualities in themselves and others  
✓ Discuss themes of bullying and standing up for what's right  
✓ Connect to animal characters and learn about wildlife  

---

## Pre-Reading Activities

### Activity 1: Predict the Story
Before reading, show students the book cover and discuss:
- What do you think this story is about?
- What might the title "Doodles and the Backyard Birds" mean?
- Who do you think the main characters are?
- What do you predict will happen?

### Activity 2: Explore the Themes
Introduce three key words:
- **Friendship** - Working together, helping others, loyalty
- **Bravery** - Standing up for what's right, facing fears
- **Standing Together** - Teamwork, unity, community

Ask students to share examples they've seen of each theme.

## Discussion Questions

### During & After Reading

**About Sheldon:**
- Why do you think Sheldon felt different from other birds?
- How did Sheldon's special feet help him?
- What made Sheldon brave?
- When do you feel like Sheldon—different or left out?

**About Friendship:**
- Who were Sheldon's true friends? How did they help him?
- Why were Gracie and Rosie (the golden doodles) good friends to Sheldon?
- What makes someone a good friend?
- Have you ever had a friend who was very different from you?

**About Bravery:**
- What was scary for Sheldon?
- How is being brave different from being fearless?
- When did Sheldon show courage?
- Can you think of a time you were brave?

**About the Bullies:**
- Why do you think the crow and grackle were bullies?
- How did Sheldon handle being bullied?
- What should you do if you see someone being bullied?

**About Standing Together:**
- How did the characters work together to solve the problem?
- What could each character do that was special?
- Why is it harder for one person to solve a problem alone?
- What happens when a community stands together?

## Classroom Activities

### Activity 1: Character Mapping
**Objective:** Understand character relationships and traits

**Materials:** Paper, markers, whiteboard

**Instructions:**
1. Draw a circle for each character: Sheldon, Gracie, Rosie, Mayor Robbins, Sandi Robbins, Gypsy, Sara, Crow, Grackle
2. Have students draw lines connecting characters who interact
3. Write character traits around each name
4. Discuss which characters are brave, kind, loyal, etc.

### Activity 2: "If I Were..." Writing
**Objective:** Build empathy and perspective-taking

**Instructions:**
Students complete one or more prompts:
- If I were Sheldon, I would feel _____ because _____
- If I were Gracie or Rosie, I would help by _____
- If I were Mayor Robbins, I would tell the community _____
- If I could help animals like the characters do, I would _____

### Activity 3: Stand Together Poster
**Objective:** Reinforce teamwork and unity themes

**Materials:** Large paper, markers, magazines, glue

**Instructions:**
1. Divide class into groups
2. Each group creates a poster showing how characters stood together
3. Include illustrations, quotes, and why teamwork matters
4. Display in classroom

### Activity 4: Animal Research
**Objective:** Connect to real animals and learn about wildlife

**Instructions:**
Have students research one of the animals in the story:
- **Golden Doodles** - Breed characteristics, temperament, why they're good companions
- **Robins, Sparrows, or other backyard birds** - Habitat, diet, behavior, how to attract them
- **Crows & Grackles** - Behavior, intelligence, natural role in ecosystems

**Presentation:** Students share findings with the class

### Activity 5: Create Your Own Animal Story
**Objective:** Develop creative writing and understanding of themes

**Instructions:**
Students write and/or illustrate their own short animal story featuring:
- A main character with something special/different about them
- A challenge or conflict
- Friends who help
- A resolution showing friendship, bravery, and teamwork

### Activity 6: Kindness & Courage Pledge
**Objective:** Connect story themes to student behavior

**Instructions:**
1. Discuss what it means to be kind, brave, and a good friend
2. Have each student write a personal pledge:
   - "I will show friendship by _____"
   - "I will be brave by _____"
   - "I will stand with others by _____"
3. Display pledges in the classroom

## Discussion: Real-World Connections

### Bullying & Standing Up
- How do real friendships help us handle difficult situations?
- What does it mean to stand up for someone who's being treated unfairly?
- How can we be like Sheldon, Gracie, and Rosie at school?

### Differences & Acceptance
- What makes each person unique and special?
- Why are differences important?
- How can we appreciate what makes people different?

### Helping Animals & Nature
- How can we help animals in our own communities?
- What role do birds play in nature?
- How can we create a welcoming space for backyard wildlife?

### Dreams & Goals (Val's Animal Refuge Mission)
- Val dreams of opening an animal refuge. What is a dream of yours?
- What steps can we take to help animals?
- How do small actions add up to big change?

## Extension Activities

### Art Projects
- Draw your favorite scene from the book
- Create a character portrait of Sheldon or other main characters
- Design a poster promoting the book or animal welfare

### Drama & Role-Play
- Act out key scenes from the story
- Perform a readers' theater version
- Create a puppet show with the characters

### Writing Projects
- Write a letter to Sheldon offering advice or encouragement
- Create a newspaper article about the bird community's victory
- Write an interview with Val C. about why she wrote the book

### STEM Connection
- Research backyard bird species and create a field guide
- Design a bird feeder or bird-friendly garden
- Study animal behavior and create an observation journal

### Service Learning
- Create a fundraiser for an animal shelter or wildlife refuge
- Organize a "Help Animals Day" at school
- Write letters to local conservation organizations

## Standards Alignment

**Common Core Standards:**
- **RL.1.1** - Retell stories and demonstrate understanding of the main message
- **RL.1.3** - Describe characters, settings, and events
- **RL.2.1** - Ask and answer questions about key details
- **RL.2.3** - Describe how characters respond to events
- **W.1.1** - Write opinion pieces
- **W.2.3** - Write narratives and recount events

**Social-Emotional Learning (SEL):**
- Self-awareness (understanding feelings and identity)
- Social awareness (empathy, perspective-taking)
- Relationship skills (cooperation, teamwork)
- Responsible decision-making (standing up for what's right)
